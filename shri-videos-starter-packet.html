How to use this packet

Part 2

What Shri Videos is

A cinematic, respectful film series about Shrinathji and the Pushtimarg tradition, made for families everywhere, including people who have never heard of Pushtimarg.

The long-term plan is about 52 weekly episodes of 12 to 15 minutes, told in order, with short pieces (30 to 60 seconds) and 3-minute stories along the way. AI tools help make the pictures and sound. People make every important decision.

The standard

The finished work must feel like a real film, not "good for AI." A shot is rejected if it shows an obvious AI mistake, a continuity error, a religious or historical error, a weak performance, or a cheap look.

The order of priorities

Story first. Emotion second. Beauty third. Teaching lives inside the story, never as a lecture. A viewer who forgets half the names should still understand who wants what, what choice is made, and why it matters.

Where Pilot 01 fits

The project files planned a short proof film before anything longer. Dr. Shah has now set the first target as a 2-minute pilot in two weeks. Pilot 01 is an internal test film. It is not published until it passes all three approval gates and Dr. Shah decides. Speed is welcome; skipping a check is not.

The one rule above all others

AI is never the religious authority. Keep what history documents separate from what devotional tradition remembers, label every claim, and never invent sacred details because a tool can draw them.

Part 4

The ten rules

These come from the project's rights policy and research framework. They apply to everything you do.

  1. Label every claim. Each script line is HISTORY, TRADITION, THEOLOGY, DRAMATIZATION or OPEN. Tradition is never presented as documented history, and never called "myth."
  2. Cite the page. Every TRADITION or HISTORY line names its source ID and page. No page, no line.
  3. Never invent sacred details. No made-up divine dialogue, dates, clothing, jewellery, architecture or miracles. If the source does not say it, the film does not say it.
  4. Sacred mantras stay out. No mantra text in scripts, subtitles, prompts or audio without Dr. Shah's written approval.
  5. No exact Gregorian dates for traditional events. Use the traditional Hindu date or no date until the calendar question is settled.
  6. Lawful access only. Never bypass a login, paywall or DRM. "Downloadable" does not mean "free to use."
  7. Owning a file is not permission to use it. Record rights and sacred status separately for every source. Archive privately; publish nothing without a rights decision.
  8. Originals are evidence. Never edit, rename inside, or overwrite a downloaded original. OCR text, notes and translations are separate files.
  9. Everything gets an ID and a log. Sources, characters, places, shots and takes all have IDs, and every AI generation that you keep is logged.
  10. Tag the period of every image. Later Nathdwara paintings are not evidence for what Braj looked like around 1500. Mark each reference with its date and use.

Step by step, for each file

  1. Pick the row in the manifest CSV. Mark it "in progress."
  2. Label it. For this upload use rights RIGHTS_UNKNOWN and sacred status INTERNAL_RESEARCH_ONLY, unless the book is clearly very old (note the year) or clearly modern.
  3. Download using the link in the download_url column.
  4. Check the MD5. In Windows PowerShell: Get-FileHash -Algorithm MD5 "file.pdf". It must equal the md5 column. If not, download again.
  5. Make the SHA-256 fingerprint: Get-FileHash -Algorithm SHA256 "file.pdf". Give the source the next ID: SHR-SRC-000001, SHR-SRC-000002 and so on.
  6. Add the registry row in SHRI_SOURCE_REGISTRY: ID, title, author or publisher, year, language, script, URL, access date, method ("archive.org download"), SHA-256, size, page count, scan or text, rights, sacred status, Drive location.
  7. Save to Drive in 10_RESEARCH_LIBRARY/01_ORIGINAL_SOURCES/AQ-0001/. Never change the file after that.

For the 10 GB stage and beyond, Yash will set up a small download script so you do not do thousands of files by hand. Your job then is checking, labelling and recording.

Part 10

  
Storytelling: what our research found

  
The team studied successful AI films, weak religious dramas and long-form documentaries. These are the lessons that became project rules.


  
The big lessons

  
    
    
  • Spectacle is not story. The Dor Brothers' APEX (3 minutes 15 seconds) was made in about ten hours and went viral. It proves AI can make impressive shots fast. It does not prove that a long story stays clear, consistent and accurate. Our advantage has to be story, emotion, accuracy and continuity.
    • 
    
  • Work on the story before scaling up. The makers of NEXUS, a 5-minute AI film made by 3 people in 2 weeks, spent about three weeks on an earlier 3-minute script and then changed the idea. Fast pictures do not remove the need for a good script.
    • 
    
  • Their working order: check the story first, lock the reference images, explore cheaply in stills, and only then compare video tools. We copy that order.
    • 
    
  • One focused story. Build around one person, one event or one question, the way Amar Chitra Katha comics do, instead of an encyclopedia of names and dates.
    • 
    
  • Learn from weak productions too. A weak recent fantasy series (Merlin) became a list of what to avoid: confusing introductions, fights that go on too long, fake crowds, modern-model casting, forced old-fashioned speech, and stories that wander away from their title.
    • 
  


  
The twelve story rules

  

    

      
RuleWhat it means for Pilot 01

      
        
1. Clarity over complexityViewers follow motive, relationship and consequence, not a list of names.

        
2. Focused entry pointOne cow, one family, one hill, one mystery.

        
3. Title promiseIf the film is called "The mystery on Govardhan hill," the hill and the mystery appear in the first ten seconds.

        
4. Character loadIntroduce a person only when they matter, with a role: "the cowherd's son," "the old woman."

        
5. Mystery, not confusionThe viewer understands the question and wants the answer. Confusion about who or where is a failure.

        
6. Moral and emotional spineDevotion, wonder, care for the animals: a feeling people recognise.

        
7. Natural languagePlain, clear English. No fake "thee and thou." Keep Sanskrit or Braj words only when they carry real meaning.

        
8. PacingEvery shot moves the story. Cut anything that stops changing the stakes.

        
9. Believable worldReal village people, real work clothes, real bodies and ages. No glossy modern-model look.

        
10. Crowd realismAvoid AI crowds where faces are visible. A few real-looking villagers beat a cloned crowd.

        
11. Learn from failuresKeep a short list of what looks fake or confusing, and check against it.

        
12. The names testA first-time viewer who forgets half the names still understands the story.

      
    

  


  
How to write each line

  
    
    
  • Use the safe wording from the research files: "Pushtimarg tradition remembers…", "Pushtimarg teaches…". Never "On [date], Shrinathji appeared and said…".
    • 
    
  • Introduce a term once, then trust the viewer. "Darshan, the sacred moment of seeing the Lord." After that, just "darshan."
    • 
    
  • Do not sound apologetic or sectarian. Explain what Pushtimarg holds without putting down other traditions.
    • 
    
  • Avoid "idol." Use "svarupa" or "sacred form."
    • 
    
  • Label beside every line in your script document, for example [TRADITION · SHR-SRC-000001 p.12].
    • 
  


  
Formats the team liked

  

    
Focused event story
One event told with mystery, emotion and an ending that asks "what happens next?" This is Pilot 01.

    
Minute by minute
From a popular AI documentary about the dinosaur extinction: strong opening moment, exact place, step-by-step progress, climax, aftermath. Good for later episodes where the order of events is the drama.

  


  
The audience test

  
The finished pilot will be shown to four kinds of viewers: committed Vaishnavs, younger Vaishnavs, Hindus who do not know Pushtimarg, and people who know nothing about the tradition. The questions: What did you feel? What confused you? What looked fake? Would you watch the next one? If people talk about the AI, the pilot failed. If they ask "what happens next?", it passed.

Software: what our research found

  
The team looked at dozens of AI film tools from the US, China and Korea. The main finding: no single product does the whole job, and tools change every few months. So we keep what matters in our own files and treat tools as rented and replaceable.


  

  
What the research learned about tools

  
    
    
  1. Reference first. Every serious tool (Google Flow, Runway, LTX Studio) works better with clean, approved reference images of each character, place and object, reused across shots.
    2. 
    
  2. Approve a still, then animate it. Make the frame, approve it, turn it into video, and assemble the clips in your own editor. A bad shot can then be replaced on its own.
    3. 
    
  3. Be careful with one-click "director" modes. User reports on long automatic generations describe faces, props and blocking drifting, and credits running out fast. Not for whole films yet.
    4. 
    
  4. Compare with identical inputs. Give two tools the same references and the same shot spec, then score both. Never pick a tool from a demo reel or a social media post.
    5. 
    
  5. Measure cost per usable second, counting failed and rejected takes, not the advertised price.
    6. 
    
  6. Hybrid beats prompt-only. Professional AI films (for example Next Stop Paris) still use normal editing, compositing and planning around the AI shots.
    7. 
    
  7. Look beyond English-language tools. Strong models come from China (Seedance, Kling, Wan, Hailuo, Vidu, HunyuanVideo) as well as the US.
    8. 
    
  8. Stop collecting tools. On 21 September the team decided there are enough candidates. The job now is controlled testing and real production.
    9.

Part 12

  
Visual canon and continuity

  
"Canon" means the approved look of every recurring person, place and object. Once something is approved and shown, it must look the same every time it returns, in Pilot 01 and in every later episode.

  
What goes in the reference pack

  

    
People SHR-CHAR-###
  • Front, three-quarter and side views
  • Age, build, hair, face, skin, scars
  • Expressions: calm, worried, amazed

    
The cow SHR-CHAR-###
  • Colour, markings, horns, size
  • Bell, rope, any ornaments
  • Side view, walking, standing still

    
Places SHR-LOC-###
  • Govardhan hillside: rocks, plants, paths
  • The village: houses, walls, roofs, wells
  • Views from several angles and times of day

    
Clothing and objects SHR-PROP-###
  • Everyday clothes of Braj villagers
  • Milk pots, ropes, baskets, tools
  • Period notes for each

  

  
The world must stay the same

  
The workflow from the production file: historical evidence → approved reconstruction → reference package → master reference images → shot generation → continuity check → reuse in every later appearance. Where nobody knows exactly how something looked, label it "historically plausible reconstruction," never "this is how it was."

  
Period tags

  
Every reference image gets a tag: what it shows, when it was made, and what it may be used for. A 19th-century Nathdwara painting is good evidence for 19th-century Nathdwara painting. It is not evidence for what a Braj village looked like around 1500. The Metropolitan Museum's open-access images (queue item AQ-0003) are free to use (CC0) and a good place to start, but tag them carefully.

  
Reject a shot if it has

  
    
    
  • Faces, hands or jewellery that change between shots.
    • 
    
  • Random letters, signs or writing in the picture.
    • 
    
  • Buildings that could not exist, or styles from the wrong century.
    • 
    
  • Modern objects, modern haircuts, modern-model bodies.
    • 
    
  • A cloned crowd: repeated faces, everyone moving the same way.
    • 
    
  • Clothing or a place that changes without a reason.
    •

The shot spec (one per shot)

  
Shot ID · length in seconds · story beat it serves · framing (wide, medium, close) · camera move · action, with times ("0–3 s: cow walks left to right; 3–6 s: stops, looks uphill") · people and animals (reference IDs) · clothing and props (IDs) · location (ID) · style frame · light and time of day · claim label and source ID · must keep · must avoid.


  
The take log (one row per kept take)

  
Take ID · shot ID · date and time · tool and model, with version if shown · prompt (copied exactly) · reference images used · settings (seed, length, aspect) · credits used · output file name · kept or rejected, and why. Rejected takes only need a count per shot.

  
Why the logs matterThe logs are how we compare tools fairly, work out the real cost per usable second, and remake a shot months later with a better tool.

Quality checks before you send anything

  

    

      
Before the script goes to Gate 1

      
    
        
  • Every line has a label and, if needed, a source ID and page.
    • 
        
  • No invented divine words, no mantra, no exact Gregorian date.
    • 
        
  • Safe wording used for tradition.
    • 
        
  • Every name arrives with a role.
    • 
        
  • About 230 to 260 words.
    • 
        
  • Read it aloud: does it make you want the next line?
    • 
      

    

    

      
Before the look goes to Gate 2

      
    
        
  • Every reference has an ID and a period tag.
    • 
        
  • Nothing modern, nothing from the wrong century.
    • 
        
  • People look like real villagers, not models.
    • 
        
  • The tool test scores are filled in for both tools.
    • 
        
  • Spend so far is under the cap.
    • 
      

    

    

      
Before the review cut (all 3 gates)

      
    
        
  • Opening hooks within 10 seconds.
    • 
        
  • Each person, the cow and the hill look the same in every shot.
    • 
        
  • No artifacts, no stray text, no cloned faces.
    • 
        
  • Narration clear; sacred terms pronounced correctly.
    • 
        
  • Music does not drown the voice.
    • 
        
  • Length 1:50 to 2:10; subtitles match the audio.
    • 
        
  • Watch it once on a phone and once on a large screen.

Part 15

  
Words to know

  
Pushtimarg terms with the plain wording our glossary recommends, then the production terms used in this packet.

  

    
Pushti
Divine grace.

    
Pushtimarg
"The Path of Grace," the tradition founded by Shri Vallabhacharyaji.

    
Shrinathji / Shreeji
The Krishna svarupa at the heart of Pushtimarg, associated with Govardhan and now in Nathdwara. Use "Shrinathji" first.

    
Mahaprabhuji
First use: "Shri Vallabhacharyaji, revered in Pushtimarg as Mahaprabhuji."

    
Gusainji
First use: "Shri Vitthalnathji, known as Gusainji," Vallabhacharyaji's son.

    
Vallabhkul
Vallabhacharyaji's lineage.

    
Seva
Loving service and care offered to the Lord.

    
Svarupa
The sacred form of the Lord. Use instead of "idol."

    
Prakatya
Manifestation, the Lord's appearing.

    
Darshan
The sacred encounter of seeing the Lord.

    
Haveli
A Pushtimarg temple, built like a home.

    
Thakurji
The Lord as cared for in the home or haveli.

    
Brahmasambandha
The sacred bond of dedication to Shri Krishna. Its mantra is never shown or spoken in our films without approval.

    
Bhava
Devotional feeling.

    
Lila
Divine play.

    
Shuddhadvaita
Vallabhacharyaji's philosophy of pure non-dualism.

    
Varta
Devotional story literature, such as the 84 and 252 Vaishnav Vartas and the Prakatya Varta. Sacred within the tradition; written down in layers over time.

    
Braj
The region around Mathura, Vrindavan, Gokul and Govardhan.

  

  

    
Gate
An approval point. Gate 1 religious and historical, Gate 2 creative, Gate 3 technical and continuity.

    
Reference pack
Approved images that define how people, places and objects look.

    
Keyframe
An approved still image for one shot, made before the video.

    
Take
One attempt from an AI tool. Most takes are thrown away.

    
Version
A saved, reviewable state of a shot or file: v001, v002.

    
Shot spec
The written instructions for one shot (see Part 13).

    
Manifest
The approved list of files to download.

    
Registry
The sheet with one row for every source we hold.

    
MD5 / SHA-256
Fingerprints of a file. If one byte changes, the fingerprint changes.

    
OCR
Turning a scanned page into typed text.

    
Claim label
HISTORY, TRADITION, THEOLOGY, DRAMATIZATION or OPEN, written beside each script line.
